Contacts

  • Press Ctrl+3 or click People at the bottom of the folder pane.
  • Change View (Home) switches between People, Business Card, Phone and List.
  • The search box above the list matches names, companies, addresses and phone numbers. The A–Z index beside the list jumps to a letter.
  • New Contact is Ctrl+Shift+C. Double-click a contact to edit it. Delete sends it to Deleted Items, after asking.
  • Merge Duplicates (Home) finds contacts that are the same person (the same email address, or the same name when their addresses don’t tell them apart) and lists each set, checked. Merge keeps the fullest contact of each set, fills in what it’s missing from the others, adds their extra email addresses and notes, and moves the others to Deleted Items.
  • Full Name beside the first name opens Check Full Name, as in Outlook: the title (Dr., Mr., Ms.…), first, middle and last name, and the suffix (Jr., Sr., III…). A name typed or picked whole, such as “Dr. John Q. Public Jr.”, is split into those parts for you. Importing and exporting CSV and vCard files keeps all five.
  • Map It under the addresses shows the address on the tab you’re on (Business, Home or Other) on a map in your web browser.
  • General, Details and All Fields on the contact form switch its pages, as in Outlook. General has the name, email, phones, addresses and notes; Details the manager, assistant, nickname, spouse, web page, IM address, birthday and anniversary. All Fields lists the fields in a Name and Value table; pick a list under Select from (Frequently-used fields, Name, Address, Email, Phone number, Personal, Miscellaneous or All Contact fields) and type a value there to change it.
  • Business Card on the contact form shows the contact’s card as it looks when you forward it, picture and all. Save As keeps it as a vCard (.vcf) file.
  • Drag a contact onto Mail at the bottom of the folder pane to write to them, onto Calendar for a new meeting with them invited, or onto Tasks for a new task named after them, with their details in its notes. A contact without an email address goes only to Tasks. Nothing is saved until you send or save the new item.
  • Forward on the contact form saves the contact and opens a new message with it attached as a business card (.vcf), as Forward Contact does.
  • Follow Up on the contact form flags the contact (Today, Tomorrow, This Week, Next Week or No Date), marks the flag complete or clears it, as Follow Up on the Home tab does. The flag shows in a line at the top of the form and is saved with the contact.
  • Address Book (Ctrl+Shift+B) under the email boxes picks the contact’s email addresses from Select Names. Check Names (Ctrl+K) turns a name typed in an email box, such as “megan”, into that person’s address; saving does the same.
  • Zoom beside the contact’s Notes makes the notes’ text larger or smaller, from 50% to 200%.
  • Saving a contact with the same name or email address as one you have shows Duplicate Contact Detected, as in Outlook: Add new contact keeps both, and Update information of selected Contact adds what you typed to the one you have.
  • Contact folders appear under My Contacts. New Folder (Home) adds one. An account added beside yours lists its contacts there too, as “Contacts - address”.
  • File → Options → People sets the sort order and how names are filed.

Contact groups

New Contact Group (Ctrl+Shift+L) gives a name to several people. Type that name in To, Cc or Bcc to send to everyone in it. On the group’s form, Add Members adds the names or addresses you type, and Address Book (Ctrl+Shift+B) adds the people you pick, from your contacts or your organization’s directory. Email and Meeting save the group, then start a message or a meeting with its members, and Forward Group sends each member as a business card (vCard). These groups are kept on this PC. Outlook’s own contact groups don’t appear, as Microsoft 365 doesn’t share them with other apps.

Importing and exporting contacts and calendars

File → Open & Export → Import/Export opens the Import and Export Wizard, as in Outlook. Pick what to do, then Next:

  • Import contacts from a Comma Separated Values file (.csv): a file exported from classic Outlook, Gmail, Outlook.com or a spreadsheet. InCloseMail reads Outlook’s column names (First Name, E-mail Address, Business Phone and so on) and Gmail’s.
  • Import a vCard file (.vcf): one business card or a file of many, such as one exported from your phone or iCloud.
  • Import an iCalendar (.ics) or vCalendar file (.vcs): every event in the file goes on your calendar. vCalendar is the older format that older phones, Lotus Notes and older versions of Outlook save. A .vcs file you open (File → Open & Export → Open Item) or get as an attachment opens as an event too, as an .ics file does. Repeating events come in as a series when Outlook could make that series too; the rest come in once, and the summary says how many. Events already on your calendar (the same subject and start) aren’t added again, and no reminders are set.
  • Export contacts to a Comma Separated Values file (.csv) saves your Contacts in Outlook’s columns, for Excel, Gmail or another program.

When you import contacts, Options says what happens to someone you already have (the same name or email address): Replace duplicates with items imported (the default) adds the file’s details to that contact and keeps what the file leaves empty, Allow duplicates to be created adds them anyway, and Do not import duplicate items leaves them out. Contacts go into your main Contacts folder, and events into your main calendar. Importing needs changes allowed.

Address Book

Ctrl+Shift+B opens the Address Book. It shows your contacts or, for a work or school account, the Global Address List. If the permission to read names and addresses wasn’t granted when you signed in, Allow asks for it. Properties shows the selected person’s contact card.

Contact cards

Click a name in a message (the sender, or someone on the To or Cc line), in the reading pane or the message’s own window, to see the person’s contact card, as in Outlook. For someone in your Contacts, it shows everything you keep about them: title and company, email addresses, phone numbers, addresses, office and birthday. For anyone else, it shows their name and address, and their picture if they’re a co-worker. Email and Schedule a Meeting start a message or meeting with them, Add to Contacts (or Edit Contact) opens the contact form, and Messages from finds their mail in your mailbox.

People peek and Favorites

Rest the mouse on People at the bottom of the folder pane for the People peek, as in Outlook. Type in Search People to find a contact by name, company or email address, and below it are your Favorites. Click a person for their contact card. To add someone to Favorites, right-click them in People (or in the peek’s search results) and choose Add to Favorites; Remove from Favorites on a favorite’s right-click menu takes them off. Favorites are kept on this PC. The pin at the top of the peek keeps People in the To-Do Bar (View → To-Do Bar → People), beside your mail.

More

  • Search People (Home) finds someone by name or address from Mail too.
  • Mail Merge (People → Home) sends one message per contact, with merge fields.
  • File → Print prints the contacts shown (search first to print only some) in Card Style, Small Booklet Style, Medium Booklet Style, Memo Style (every field and the notes) or Phone Directory Style (names and numbers), with a preview. Page Setup sets the style’s fonts, gray shading, paper, margins, header and footer (see Page Setup).
  • Forward Contact sends a contact as a card, Contact Groups lists your groups, and Delete Folder removes a contacts folder you made.
  • Open Shared Contacts shows contacts someone has shared with you.
  • As in Outlook, the Contacts of each shared mailbox in your folder pane is listed under My Contacts (“Contacts - support@…”). Its contacts are read when you open it, and can be read but not changed here.
  • Follow Up flags a contact, as in Outlook.
