InCloseMail can ask for a second step before it opens your mail: Windows Hello (your face, fingerprint or PIN) or a code from an authenticator app on your phone. Then someone at your unlocked PC, or a program running as you, can’t read your mail in InCloseMail without it.

Turn it on

  1. Go to File › Options › Trust Center › Lock InCloseMail and click Turn On. (The first-run wizard offers it too.)
  2. Choose how to unlock:
    • Windows Hello (recommended). It locks InCloseMail’s window, AI agents’ access and the copy of your mail on this PC: the files are encrypted with a key only Windows Hello (or your recovery words) can open. Windows asks for your face, fingerprint or PIN up to three times while it’s set up. If Windows Hello isn’t set up on the PC, set it up first in Windows Settings › Accounts › Sign-in options.
    • A code from an authenticator app (Microsoft Authenticator, Google Authenticator or another). It locks the window and AI agents’ access only. The copy of your mail on this PC stays protected by your Windows sign-in alone, because a code can’t lock files: checking it needs a secret that’s on the PC too. In your app, add an account and scan the QR code InCloseMail shows. If you can’t scan it, choose to enter a setup key instead and type the key shown below the code.
  3. Choose when it locks. It always asks when InCloseMail starts, and also, if you like, when Windows locks (Windows+L, or the screen locking by itself) and after a number of minutes without use.
  4. Write down your 12 recovery words, on paper, in order, and keep them somewhere safe away from the PC. They’re the only way in if you lose your way to unlock: a new PC, Windows Hello reset, a lost phone. InCloseMail shows them once.

With Windows Hello, InCloseMail starts again to finish, and you sign in once more, in your browser: with the lock on, InCloseMail keeps its own sign-in, locked, rather than leaving it with Windows (see what it can’t lock).

When it’s locked

  • InCloseMail shows InCloseMail is locked before any mail. With Windows Hello, Windows asks at once; with an authenticator app, type the 6-digit code it shows.
  • Locked means paused: InCloseMail doesn’t sync or show new-mail alerts until it’s unlocked. When it locks while you’re writing, the message is saved to Drafts, and InCloseMail says so when you unlock.
  • AI agents (Claude and others) are told “InCloseMail is locked; unlock it to continue”, and get nothing until you unlock it.
  • Lost your way to unlock? Click Use your recovery words instead. Afterward InCloseMail asks you to set up a new way to unlock and gives you new words.
  • Lost the words too? Start over on this PC deletes this PC’s copy of your mail and your sign-in, and turns the lock off. Your mail is still on the server: sign in again and it comes back. Messages in the Outbox and changes not sent yet are lost.

To change how it unlocks, get new recovery words, or turn it off, use Change or Turn Off in the same place. Each asks for your current way to unlock first. Lock Now locks it at once.

Locking your mail down everywhere

The lock protects InCloseMail on this PC. Your mail itself lives in Microsoft 365 or Outlook.com, and anything else signed in to your account can still read it: Outlook on the web in a browser, Outlook on your phone, another PC. So, to really lock your mail down:

  1. Make InCloseMail the only way in. Sign out of your mail everywhere else and, if you can, have the other ways turned off.
  2. Make Microsoft’s own sign-in strong. Every new sign-in, in a browser or an app, should need MFA, ideally a passkey or Windows Hello rather than a code.
  3. Turn on InCloseMail’s lock with Windows Hello, and have it lock when Windows locks.

What you can do yourself

  1. Sign out of Outlook on the web in every browser (outlook.office.com, outlook.live.com), on every PC you use. Sign out, don’t just close the tab: a closed tab keeps you signed in.
  2. Don’t choose “Stay signed in?” when a browser asks after a Microsoft sign-in. It keeps the browser signed in for weeks without asking again.
  3. Don’t sign your browser’s profile in to your work account (Edge: Settings › Profiles). A signed-in profile signs in to Outlook on the web by itself.
  4. End every other sign-in. Work or school account: My Sign-Ins › Security info, sign out everywhere. Personal account: account.microsoft.com › Security, sign out everywhere. InCloseMail is signed out too; sign it in again afterward.
  5. Remove your mail from devices you don’t need it on: mail apps on your phone and tablet, Outlook on other PCs, other mail programs.
  6. Use strong MFA on your account: a passkey (in Microsoft Authenticator or a security key) or Windows Hello for Business. These can’t be phished the way a texted code or an approve/deny prompt can.
  7. Delete app passwords if you ever made any. They skip MFA entirely.
  8. Check where your mail goes: no forwarding to an outside address you didn’t set up (InCloseMail’s Rules and Alerts, or Outlook on the web’s settings), and no apps you don’t recognize allowed to read your mail.

Outlook.com, Hotmail and other personal accounts have no admin switches, so it’s this list: also consider going passwordless (account.microsoft.com › Security › Advanced security options), and turn off POP (Outlook.com › Settings › Mail › Forwarding and IMAP) if you don’t use it.

For IT admins (Microsoft 365 work or school accounts)

These need an Exchange Online or Entra ID admin. Try them on a test mailbox first.

Turn off the other ways into the mailbox. InCloseMail reads and sends mail through Microsoft Graph, which isn’t one of Exchange Online’s per-mailbox switches, so it should keep working with the others off. In Exchange Online PowerShell:

$off = @{
    OWAEnabled                       = $false  # Outlook on the web
    OneWinNativeOutlookEnabled       = $false  # new Outlook for Windows
    MAPIEnabled                      = $false  # classic Outlook for Windows and Mac
    ActiveSyncEnabled                = $false  # phone mail apps (Exchange ActiveSync)
    OutlookMobileEnabled             = $false  # Outlook for iOS and Android
    PopEnabled                       = $false
    ImapEnabled                      = $false
    EwsEnabled                       = $false  # Exchange Web Services programs
    SmtpClientAuthenticationDisabled = $true   # leave SMTP allowed if InCloseMail sends through SMTP
}
Set-CASMailbox -Identity user@contoso.com @off

Then sign in to InCloseMail on the test mailbox and read, send and move a message. Check other ways to reach your mailbox (File › Options › Advanced) will show IMAP and SMTP as unavailable; that’s expected. If shared mailboxes stop appearing by themselves, add them by hand (right-click your mailbox › Add Shared Mailbox). A softer option than turning Outlook on the web off is app enforced restrictions: read-only, no downloads in browsers on unmanaged PCs.

Make browser sign-ins short-lived and strong (Conditional Access, Entra ID P1):

  • Require authentication strength › Phishing-resistant MFA (passkeys, Windows Hello for Business, certificates).
  • For Office 365 with client apps Browser: Sign-in frequency (for example 1 hour) and Persistent browser session › Never persistent. Limited to browsers, InCloseMail isn’t asked to sign in every hour.
  • Block the device code flow, turn off app passwords, don’t let users consent to unknown apps reading mail, and block automatic forwarding to outside addresses.

Afterward, end sessions that were already signed in: Entra admin center › the user › Revoke sessions. InCloseMail asks to sign in again once. On Entra-joined PCs, Windows itself gives browsers single sign-on, so the Windows sign-in is part of the lock too: require Windows Hello for Business and a short screen lock.

What it can’t lock

  • InCloseMail while it’s unlocked. A program running as you can read what InCloseMail shows. Locking when Windows locks, or after a few idle minutes, keeps that time short.
  • Windows’ own sign-in. When InCloseMail signs in through Windows, Windows keeps the sign-in where InCloseMail’s lock can’t reach it. So with the lock on Windows Hello, InCloseMail signs in through the browser and keeps its sign-in locked, unless your organization’s policy says to sign in through Windows.
  • Mail you saved or exported (PST and .mbox files, Export for AI, attachments you saved). Those are files of their own.
  • Pictures in messages that InCloseMail’s message view has downloaded and keeps in its own browser cache.
  • Your Microsoft 365 admins and Microsoft itself can still reach the mailbox.

To check it works: lock the PC and unlock it; InCloseMail asks for Windows Hello before showing any mail.
