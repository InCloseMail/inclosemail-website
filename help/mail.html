Reading mail

  • Click a message to read it in the reading pane. Double-click it, or press Enter, to open it in its own window.
  • Use View → Reading Pane to put the pane on the right, at the bottom, or turn it off.
  • View → View Settings → Other Settings sets the list’s column, row and message preview fonts, adds grid lines between rows, and can always use the single-line or the compact layout, wherever the reading pane is.
  • Pictures from the web are blocked until you click Download pictures, so senders can’t tell when you open their mail. Always download from beside it adds the sender, or their whole domain, to your Safe Senders, and pictures in Safe Senders’ mail download by themselves (File → Options → Trust Center → Automatic Download turns that off). Pictures are fetched from the web each time you read the message; they don’t take up space in your copy of the mail.
  • Files that can run code, such as .exe and .js files, are blocked, as they are in Outlook.
  • Dark Mode shows messages dark, with their photos as sent and their charts and report pictures dark too. Mail designed dark, on a black or navy page, is shown as it is. The sun at the right of the message header (Switch Background) shows a message as it was sent. In light mode it’s the other way around: mail designed dark is shown on a white page, its photos as sent. To keep every message’s own colors, check File → Options → General → Never change the message background color.
  • In a work or school account, a dot on the sender’s picture shows whether a co-worker is Available, Busy, Away or Offline in Teams.
  • File → Options → Mail → Remove extra line breaks in plain text messages tidies up plain text mail, as in Outlook. Restore line breaks in the message puts them back.
  • File → Options → Mail sets when a message counts as read: right away, after waiting, or when you select another message.
  • Filter Email (Home → Find) shows only the Unread, Has Attachments, Categorized, Flagged or Important messages, or those Sent To: Me or CC: Me. Clear Filter shows them all again.
  • How the list looks: View → Change View (Compact, Single or Preview), Message Preview (off, or 1 to 3 lines), Columns, the arrangement buttons, Reverse Sort and Show in Groups. Save Current View As a New View and Manage Views keep views of your own, and View Settings → Conditional Formatting colors messages that match.
  • Quick Actions: pointing at a message in the list shows two buttons. Home → Follow Up → Set Quick Actions chooses which.
  • Quick icons: under the sun at the right of the message header, small icons do the one thing that fits the message shown, and appear only where they apply:
    • Unsubscribe on a newsletter or other mailing. InCloseMail asks first, then unsubscribes in one click where the sender allows it, or opens the sender’s unsubscribe page, or starts the unsubscribe email for you to send. Afterward it offers to delete that sender’s mail in the folder too; it goes to Deleted Items, and Undo brings it back. It isn’t offered in Junk Email or on mail that looks like phishing, where unsubscribing would only tell a spammer your address is read.
    • Restore in Deleted Items moves a message back to the folder it was deleted from, or to the Inbox when that isn’t known.
    • Move to Inbox on mail in your Spam folder (the one Move to Spam fills) or a folder inside it. Mail you filed in Archive or a folder of your own doesn’t show it.
    • Add to Contacts when the sender is a person who isn’t in your contacts yet. It opens a new contact with their name and address filled in.
    • Not Junk in Junk Email, in your own mailbox and in shared mailboxes, and Blacklist Email Address (Blacklist).
  • New mail: File → Options → Mail → Message arrival can play a sound, show a Desktop Alert (with Delete, Flag and Dismiss), put an envelope on the taskbar button, and suggest a rule for mail that arrives often, such as Teams chat notifications.
  • Alerts for other folders: Desktop Alerts are for the Inbox, as in Outlook. When your rules file mail into folders, right-click a folder → Properties and check Show a desktop alert when new mail arrives here, and new mail there pops up too. Review Settings lists the folders your rules file into, each with that box.
  • Read Aloud (Home → Speech) reads the message to you in a Windows voice. It’s also in a message’s own window and, while you write, on the Review tab, where it reads the text you selected, or the whole message. Click it again to stop.

Attachments

  • Click an attachment to preview it in place of the message (pictures, text and PDF files); other files open in their own app. Back to message, or Show Message on the ribbon, shows the message again.
  • While a file previews, the Attachments tab has Open, Quick Print, Save As, Save All Attachments, Remove Attachment and Copy, as in Outlook. Select All makes them act on every attachment of the message. The bar over the preview has the same commands, also in a message opened in its own window.
  • The arrow on an attachment, or a right-click, has the same commands for that file.
  • Save All Attachments (also Save All beside the attachments) lists them first, all checked: uncheck any you don’t want, then pick the folder.
  • Files that can run programs (such as .exe or .bat) are blocked, as in Outlook: a bar over the attachments names them, and they can’t be opened or saved.
  • Drag an attachment to a folder, the desktop or a new message to copy it there. Files you copy in File Explorer, or with an attachment’s Copy, are attached when you paste them (Ctrl+V) into a message you’re writing.
  • While writing, right-click an attached file for Open, Quick Print, Save As, Copy and Remove Attachment.
  • Attach File (Message or Insert tab of a message you’re writing) lists the files you used lately, newest first; Browse This PC opens the file dialog.
  • Drag files from File Explorer onto Mail at the bottom of the folder pane to start a new message with them attached.
  • Click a file attached to a message you’re writing to open it in its app. If you change it and save it there, the message takes the changed file, as in Outlook.
  • Mail from some Outlook setups arrives with a winmail.dat file. InCloseMail shows the files inside it instead, so you can open, save and forward them.
  • With a photo attached (JPEG, PNG or BMP), check Resize large images when I send this message under the attachments to send the pictures at most 1024 × 768, as in Outlook. Only the sent message changes: the attachments while you write, and a saved draft, keep the full-size pictures. A saved draft remembers the setting.

Focused Inbox

The Inbox of each of your accounts has three tabs. Focused and Other split your mail by Microsoft’s own classification, so each one hides the other’s messages. All shows the whole Inbox. The number beside Inbox counts everything, and the number beside Other shows how many unread messages are on that tab.

  • Right-click a message → Focused Inbox for Move to Other or Move to Focused.
  • Always Move to Other or Always Move to Focused puts that sender’s new mail on that tab everywhere, including Outlook on the web.
  • To turn the tabs off, clear View → Show Focused Inbox. Shared mailboxes and other folders show All and Unread instead.

Writing mail

  • New Email is Ctrl+N, and Ctrl+Shift+M from anywhere. Reply is Ctrl+R and Reply All Ctrl+Shift+R. Ctrl+F finds, as in other programs; to have it Forward, as in Outlook, check Ctrl+F forwards a message in File → Options → Mail.
  • A new message is From the address chosen in File → Options → Mail → Start new messages from; the From list marks it (Default), and the gear beside it opens that option. If it’s left on Automatic, it’s the mailbox at the top of the folder pane. A reply or forward is from the account the message came to. Pick another in the From list.
  • Auto-Complete: as you type a name in To, Cc or Bcc, InCloseMail suggests people from all your accounts: those you’ve written to and your contacts, the From account’s first. After a moment it also looks in your organization’s directory, so a colleague turns up even if you’ve never written to them. The first time, click To in a message from that account and allow the Address Book. Up and Down pick a suggestion, Enter or Tab takes it, and the X removes it from the list.
  • Send with Send, Ctrl+Enter or Alt+S. A message can carry up to 20 MB of attachments.
  • Drafts save themselves every 3 minutes. File → Options → Mail changes that.
  • Commenting inside the original: check File → Options → Mail → Replies and forwards → Preface comments with, and what you type into the quoted message of a reply or forward starts with [Your name] (or the text you put in the box), in blue, so the reader can tell your comments from the original.
  • Signatures: Insert → Signature → Signatures, where you also set the defaults for new messages and for replies.
  • Send later: Options → Delay Delivery. Exchange holds the message in the Outbox, so it goes out even with InCloseMail closed.
  • Quick Parts and templates: Insert → Quick Parts and Message → View Templates. Both are kept on this PC.
  • AutoCorrect fixes common typos as you type, capitalizes sentences, curls quotes, turns (c) into © and -- into a dash, starts a list from * or 1., and makes web and email addresses links. Ctrl+Z right after takes a change back. Turn each one off in File → Options → Mail → AutoCorrect and pasting.
  • Paste (Message tab) has Keep Source Formatting, Merge Formatting, Keep Text Only and Paste Special (Ctrl+Alt+V). After a paste, the (Ctrl) button beside it pastes again the way you choose.
  • Spelling & Grammar (Review tab, or F7) checks the message. File → Options → Mail → Always check spelling before sending does it every time.
  • Message Options, the small launcher at the corner of Message → Tags, sets importance, sensitivity, delivery and read receipts, and voting buttons. Insert → Table and Insert → Pictures add tables and pictures.
  • Before sending, InCloseMail warns about a reply to 20 or more people, outside recipients on a message that also goes to colleagues, and an attachment you mentioned but didn’t attach. Turn this off in File → Options → Mail.

Organizing mail

  • Delete moves messages to Deleted Items. Shift+Delete skips Deleted Items, after asking. Archive moves them to Archive; to have Backspace archive too, as in Outlook, check Backspace archives a message in File → Options → Mail.
  • Undo (Ctrl+Z) takes back your last moves, deletes, flags and categories, up to 20 of them.
  • Follow Up flags messages. Insert flags for today.
  • Categorize adds color categories. In All Categories you can give one a shortcut key.
  • Quick Steps (Home tab) run several actions on the selected messages with one click. They aren’t rules; see Once, or from now on?
  • Clean Up and Ignore (Home tab) deal with long conversations.
  • View → Show as Conversations threads the list. View → Clean Conversation View reads a whole conversation like a chat in the reading pane.
  • File → Info → Tools → Mailbox Cleanup shows how big your mailbox and its folders are, and finds old or large items to delete or archive. Archive Old Items there, and AutoArchive in File → Options → Advanced, move old mail to the Archive folder.
  • In a message’s own window, Actions → Edit Message lets you change a message you received (for example, to add notes), then Save (Ctrl+S). Actions → View in Browser opens it in your web browser. More, next to Forward, has Meeting (Ctrl+Alt+R, a meeting with everyone on the message) and Forward as Attachment (Ctrl+Alt+F). When the window is narrow, the buttons that don’t fit go to a second row.
  • File → Print (Ctrl+P) shows how the message will print before you print it.
  • Replying to an older message in a conversation says so, with a link to the latest one. File → Options → Mail can write replies in the reading pane instead of a new window (turn off Open replies and forwards in a new window).
  • Type @ and a name in a message to mention someone; they’re added to To.
  • File → Options → Mail → Tracking decides whether to send read receipts: ask each time (the default), always, or never.
  • To open email links in InCloseMail, choose it in File → Options → General → Default Programs.
  • Folders: right-click a folder → Show in Favorites to keep it at the top of the folder pane. Drag folders up or down to put them in your own order; right-click → Move & Copy → Sort Subfolders A to Z goes back to the usual order. View → Folder Pane sets the pane to Normal, Minimized or Off.
  • The Folder tab has New Folder, Rename, Copy Folder, Delete All, Run Rules Now, Folder Permissions and Folder Properties. View → Open in New Window (or right-click a folder) opens the folder in a second window.
  • Automatic Replies (File → Info) answers mail for you while you’re away.
  • Folder → Recover Deleted Items brings back what was deleted from Deleted Items, for a while afterward.

Analytics

File → Analytics shows who writes to you most, who you write to, when your mail comes and how fast you answer, for any of your accounts. Pick a period (the last 30 days, 90 days, 12 months, or all your mail) and click Analyze. You get the figures (received and sent, your usual reply time, what’s still unread), charts of your busiest hours and days, and top 10 lists of senders, companies and the people you write to. Only who each message is from and to, and when, is read, never what it says, and it’s all worked out on your PC.

Once, or from now on? Quick Steps, rules and similar commands

Some commands act once, on the messages you’ve selected. Others keep working on mail that arrives later. Point at a command to see what it does.

Once, on the selected messages

  • Quick Steps (Home tab, or right-click a message → Quick Steps) run their actions on the messages you’ve selected, when you click them: move, copy, categorize, flag, mark as read, reply, forward, delete and more. A Quick Step is not a rule: mail that arrives later isn’t touched. Point at one to see its actions.
    • The first time a Quick Step needs a folder or a category (for example, Move to: ?), it asks for one and remembers it. After that, a click moves the selected messages there at once.
    • Manage Quick Steps adds, changes and reorders them, and gives one a Ctrl+Shift shortcut.
    • The one exception is the action Always Move Messages in This Conversation: a Quick Step with it also moves later messages in that conversation (below), and its tooltip says so.
  • Move, Delete, Archive, Categorize, Follow Up and Mark as Read.
  • Clean Up Conversation moves the messages that a later message in the conversation quotes in full to Deleted Items. Clean Up Folder does it for a whole folder. File → Options → Mail → Conversation Clean Up sets the folder they go to (Deleted Items unless you pick another), whether subfolders are recreated there, and which messages stay put: unread, categorized, flagged or digitally signed.
  • Run Rules Now (in Rules and Alerts) applies your rules once, to mail already in a folder.

From now on, on new mail too

  • Rules (Create Rule, Manage Rules & Alerts): Exchange runs them on each new message, even with InCloseMail closed. See Rules.
  • Always Move Messages From (right-click a message → Rules) makes a rule that moves that sender’s new mail to a folder you pick, and moves the messages of theirs already in this folder at once.
  • Always Move Messages in This Conversation (Move menu) moves the conversation’s messages now, and new ones as they arrive. Choose it again to stop.
  • Blacklist Email Address sends the sender’s mail to Junk Email from now on (Blacklist).
  • Ignore moves a conversation, and any new messages in it, to Deleted Items. On an ignored conversation, Stop Ignoring ends it.
  • Always Move to Other and Always Move to Focused put a sender’s new mail on that tab of the Focused Inbox.
  • Script Rules run a script of yours when matching mail arrives (Script rules).

Signed and encrypted mail (S/MIME)

  • Signed and encrypted messages open in the reading pane, as in Outlook. A bar above them says who signed the message and whether the signature is valid; Details shows the certificate.
  • To sign or encrypt a message you write, use Sign and Encrypt on its Options tab. File → Options → Trust Center can turn them on for every message and lists your email certificates.
  • InCloseMail uses the certificates in Windows, as Outlook does. Your own certificate (with its private key) comes from your organization, or you import it with Manage Certificates.
  • To encrypt a message, InCloseMail needs each recipient’s certificate. It keeps the certificate of everyone who sends you signed mail; if one is missing, it asks whether to send the message unencrypted.

Microsoft 365 Groups

  • Home → Browse Groups puts the groups you belong to in the folder pane, under Groups. The first time, Microsoft asks to let InCloseMail read them; in many organizations an administrator has to approve that once.
  • Select a group to see its conversations. Each opens with its posts, newest first.
  • Reply to a conversation to post in it. New Email while a group is selected starts a new conversation. Both are sent from your own mailbox, as mail to the group.
  • Each group’s calendar is listed in Calendar with your other calendars. Check it to see it.

Search

  • Ctrl+F (or Ctrl+E) puts the cursor in the search box; with the keyboard in the reading pane, Ctrl+F finds text in that message. Results appear as you type. Esc goes back to the folder.
  • The Search tab sets the scope: Current Folder, Subfolders, Current Mailbox or All Mailboxes.
  • You can type criteria alongside words, such as from:"Megan", hasattachments:yes or read:no. The Refine buttons and Advanced Find write them in the language InCloseMail is shown in (in German, von:, hatanlagen:ja, empfangen:diese woche), and you can type them in any of its languages. English always works.
  • Advanced Find is Ctrl+Shift+F. New Search Folder is Ctrl+Shift+P: unread, flagged or important mail, mail from (or from and to) certain people, mail sent directly to you or to a group, categorized, large, old, with attachments, or with certain words.
  • File → Options → Search sets how many results show and whether Deleted Items is included.

Rules

Home → Rules → Manage Rules & Alerts lists your rules. Create Rule starts one from the selected message. On mail in an added account, it makes a rule in that account. Rules are kept and run by Exchange, so they work with InCloseMail closed and show in Outlook on the web. Run Rules Now applies them to mail already in a folder, but never forwards it. On a big folder, see Rules on big folders, and Cleaning up mail for Clean Up My Inbox. Export Rules and Import Rules move your rules to another mailbox. Rules and Alerts also has Run Selected Rule, Turn All On / Turn All Off, a search box, and two repairs: Fix Rules for My Addresses and Fix Rules That Copy Mail.

An AI agent can write and tidy your rules for you from a description in your own words; see Clean up your whole account.

Rules Wizard

Home → Rules → Rules Wizard (Organize Mail into Folders), or Rules Wizard in Rules and Alerts, looks through your mail and suggests folders to sort it into:

  • a folder for each co-worker who writes often, inside Inbox › Co-workers (by first name, if you choose);
  • a folder for each company you hear from often, whoever there writes (matched by their email domain), inside Inbox › Companies;
  • a folder for each other busy sender.

It scans your mail first: choose how far back, how many messages make a sender worth a folder, and whether to suggest only co-workers and companies you’ve written to. Then check what you want (each list has Select All and Clear All; single senders start unchecked) and click Create. It makes a rule for each folder, creates any folder that doesn’t exist yet, and, with Also file the mail already in the Inbox, moves the mail that’s already there.

Start over with your rules

When years of rules have piled up, Start Over in Rules and Alerts clears the way. It saves every rule to a backup file in Documents\InCloseMail\Rules backups, deletes them, and then offers the Rules Wizard to build a new set. Your folders and mail stay as they are. If the backup can’t be saved, nothing is deleted. To bring an old rule back, use Import Rules with the backup file.

Script rules

Home → Rules → Script Rules runs a script of yours when matching mail arrives, as classic Outlook’s “run a script” rule did, with a script file instead of VBA. A rule can watch your Inbox, an added account’s or a shared mailbox’s, and match on the sender, the subject and whether there are attachments. The script can be a PowerShell script (.ps1), a batch file (.cmd or .bat) or a program (.exe).

  • The script gets one argument: the path of a JSON file with the message’s details (subject, fromAddress, fromName, to, cc, received, hasAttachments, bodyPreview, internetMessageId, mailbox and emlPath, where the whole message is saved as an .eml file). Both files are deleted when the script ends.
  • Nothing from the message is put on the script’s command line, so a crafted subject can’t run anything.
  • PowerShell scripts run only if they were made on this PC or are signed (the RemoteSigned policy); a downloaded unsigned script doesn’t run.
  • Rules run while InCloseMail is open, on the new mail it finds, and are kept on this PC. Run Now tries a rule on the selected message.

A short example, saving each matching message as an .eml file in a folder:

param($Path)
$m = Get-Content -Raw -LiteralPath $Path | ConvertFrom-Json
Copy-Item -LiteralPath $m.emlPath -Destination "C:\Invoices\$($m.internetMessageId.Trim('<>')).eml"

Junk mail and phishing

  • Home → Junk has Block Sender, Never Block Sender, Never Block Sender’s Domain, Never Block this Group or Mailing List, Not Junk and Junk Email Options.
  • InCloseMail’s Safe Senders list is kept on this PC and works while the app is running. For filtering that works everywhere, also add senders in Outlook on the web.
  • Junk Email Options also has Safe Recipients, Blocked Senders and International, where you can block country domains (such as .ru) and mail in other alphabets. While InCloseMail is running, new mail they block goes to Junk Email; safe senders, safe recipients and your contacts always get through. Block Sender adds to Blocked Senders.
  • When a message has signs of phishing, a bar appears above it. Click Why is this suspicious? for the reasons and what to do. Block Sender and I know this sender are there too. The bar never moves or blocks anything by itself.
  • Outside mail that claims to be internal or verified, such as “verified as an internal communication”, is a strong sign of phishing, since your organization doesn’t mark mail that way.
  • In Junk Email Options → Phishing, list People to protect, such as your CEO. Mail from outside your organization that uses one of those names in the sender’s name or address is marked as possible phishing, even when the name is misspelled by a letter.
  • To turn the warning bar off, clear Warn me about messages that look like phishing on the same tab.

Blacklist

The blacklist is for senders you never want to hear from again. Their mail goes straight to Junk Email on every device, even when InCloseMail is closed, because the blacklist is a rule on your mailbox, “Blacklist (InCloseMail)”, that runs before your other rules.

  • Add a sender: open one of their messages and click Blacklist Email Address, the button at the right of the message header (under the sun in Dark Mode). It’s also in Home → Junk and on the message’s right-click Junk menu. Confirm, and that message moves to Junk Email along with everything they send from now on.
  • If a rule of yours also handles that sender (for example, an Always Move Messages From), InCloseMail shows it and asks what to do: add the sender and leave the rule alone, add the sender and delete the rule, or cancel. Deleting a rule deletes all of it, including its other senders and conditions.
  • See and change the list: Home → Junk → Manage Blacklist lists every address. Type an address and click Add, or select one and click Remove.
  • Changes need InCloseMail to be allowed to change your mailbox. The list is the same on every PC, since it lives in your mailbox; if you turn the rule off in Rules and Alerts, adding an address turns it back on.
  • Blacklist or Block Sender? Block Sender adds to Junk Email Options’ Blocked Senders, which InCloseMail applies while it’s running. The blacklist works everywhere, all the time.
