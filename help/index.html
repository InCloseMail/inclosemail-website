If you’ve used classic Outlook, most of InCloseMail is already familiar: the ribbon, the menus, the right-click options and the keyboard shortcuts are where you expect them. These pages cover getting started and the places where InCloseMail works differently.

Get started

  1. Install it. Download InCloseMail.msi and run it. You need Windows 10 (version 1809) or Windows 11, 64-bit. If message text doesn’t show after installing, see Troubleshooting.
  2. Sign in. Use a Microsoft 365 work or school account, or an Outlook.com account. The first time, Microsoft asks you to allow the app’s permissions, all of them at once, so you aren’t asked again later. Your organization may need an administrator to approve them (For administrators).
  3. Coming from classic Outlook? See Moving from classic Outlook to bring your signatures across and open your old .pst files.
  4. Start using it. Your mail, calendar and contacts are ready to use, as in Outlook. Want to look around first without changing anything? Make the account read-only; see Read-only mode.

Just want to look? Choose Try demo mode on the sign-in screen. It opens a sample mailbox, calendar and contacts that you can change freely, with no account. Already signed in? Help → Demo Mode opens the demo in a window of its own, and your account stays signed in.

Find your way around

  • Switch areas with the Mail, Calendar, People and Tasks buttons at the bottom of the folder pane, or Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+4.
  • Find any command by typing a few words in the Tell me what you want to do box to the right of the ribbon tabs (Alt+Q).
  • Open help for the area you’re in by pressing F1.

Language

InCloseMail is in English, German, French and Spanish. It starts in the language Windows uses, when it’s one of these, and in English otherwise. To pick another, go to File → Options → Language and choose Deutsch, Français, Español, English or Match Windows. InCloseMail offers to restart in it: Restart Now counts down from 5 and restarts by itself, or choose Later and it shows the next time you start. Each PC keeps its own choice. To try a language in demo mode, do the same in the demo: a demo window opened beside your account restarts in that language with a fresh demo mailbox, and your own choice stays as it is. Dates, times and numbers follow your Windows region settings, and folder names such as Inbox, Drafts and Sent Items come from your mailbox, in the language it was set up with (Microsoft keeps them, so InCloseMail can’t translate them). To change them, open Outlook on the web, go to Settings → General → Language and time, choose the language and tick Rename default folders so their names match the specified language (the Language page in Options says so too). The demo mailboxes have a few messages in German, French and Spanish too, near the top of the Inbox with the purple category. These help pages are in English.

Different from Outlook

  • Read-only when you want it. Any account can be made read-only, or allowed only some kinds of change. See Read-only mode.
  • Settings are InCloseMail’s own. Options, Quick Steps, signatures, Quick Parts, templates and Safe Senders are kept apart from Outlook’s, so Outlook keeps its own; your other PCs with InCloseMail share them (see Your settings on every PC). Rules, categories and Automatic Replies are in your mailbox and are shared with Outlook too.
  • Attachments are never stored on this PC ahead of time, and the local copy keeps 30 days of mail unless you change it. See Offline and the local copy.
  • Contact groups are InCloseMail’s own. Outlook’s contact groups don’t appear, as Microsoft 365 doesn’t share them with other apps.
  • A few things aren’t available because Microsoft doesn’t offer them to other apps, including message recall and room lists.
